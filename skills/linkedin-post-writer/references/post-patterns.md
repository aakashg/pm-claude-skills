# Post Patterns — Worked Examples

Background reference for `linkedin-post-writer`. Read before choosing a format. Study the structure, not the words.

The listicle example lives in SKILL.md. The other four formats are below.

---

## Story arc — personal transformation

Best for turning points. Optimizes for comments.

```
I quit my $200K job with no backup plan.

Everyone thought I was crazy. My parents were terrified. My friends said "just wait a year."

But I'd done the math.

I was spending 50 hours a week building someone else's vision.
I was spending 0 hours a week building my own.

The first 3 months were brutal:
- Month 1: Made $0. Questioned everything.
- Month 2: Landed one client. $2,500.
- Month 3: Three clients. $11K.

By month 6, I'd matched my old salary.
By month 12, I'd doubled it.

The difference wasn't talent or luck.

It was that I finally had skin in the game.

When your rent depends on your output, you learn fast.
When your reputation is your brand, you show up differently.

The "safe" path isn't always the safe path.
Sometimes the riskiest move is staying where you are.

What's the scariest career decision you've ever made?
```

Why it works: opens with a shocking action that creates tension. The month-by-month timeline is specific and honest — month 1 was $0, which is the detail that makes the rest believable. The closing reframes "risk" in a way the reader can apply to their own life.

## Framework / mental model

Best for teaching a concept. Optimizes for saves.

```
Every decision in my business comes down to one question:

"Does this create leverage, or does this trade time for money?"

Most solopreneurs stay stuck because they only do the second.

Here's how I think about it:

→ Writing a post = leverage (works while you sleep)
→ Doing a 1:1 call = time trade (dies when you stop)
→ Building a course = leverage (sell it 1,000 times)
→ Custom client work = time trade (sell it once)

This doesn't mean time trades are bad.

They're how you learn what to turn into leverage.

Every 1:1 client teaches you patterns.
Those patterns become frameworks.
Those frameworks become content.
That content becomes a product.

The goal isn't to stop trading time.
The goal is to turn every time trade into future leverage.

P.S. What's one thing in your business you'd love to turn into leverage?
```

Why it works: one memorable question does the framing. Four examples in a consistent `→` format make it scannable. The insight builds rather than dismisses — it reframes time trades as inputs to leverage.

## Contrarian / hot take

Best for challenging what the reader is actively doing. Optimizes for shares.

```
Unpopular opinion: Most "personal brands" are just performances.

I followed every rule:
- Post daily
- Use hooks
- Engage for 30 minutes
- Optimize for the algorithm

My engagement went up. My audience grew.

But I felt like a fraud.

Because I was writing what performed, not what I believed.

The turning point: I wrote a post about failing.

Not a humble-brag failure. A real one.

I lost a major client because I overpromised and underdelivered.

That post got fewer likes than my usual content.

But it got 47 DMs.

Real conversations. People sharing their own failures. Two of them became clients.

The "brand" posts got impressions.
The real post got relationships.

Now I write with one rule: if it makes me slightly uncomfortable to hit publish, it's probably worth posting.

What's a post you almost didn't publish?
```

Why it works: the hook challenges something the reader is doing right now, which stops the scroll. The vulnerability is specific — lost a client, why, what happened. "Impressions vs. relationships" is a reusable mental model.

## Observation

Best for a technique or trend. Fastest to write.

```
I write every post like it's a letter to one person.

Not "my audience." Not "LinkedIn." One friend.

Here's what happens when you do this:

- You stop using jargon (your friend doesn't talk like that)
- You cut the filler (you wouldn't waste their time)
- You get to the point (you know what they need to hear)
- You sound like a human (because you're writing like one)

Try this:

Before your next post, pick someone specific.

A mentee. A past version of yourself. A client you helped last month.

Write it to them. Just them.

Then delete the "Hey [name]" at the top.

That's your post.

The best content doesn't feel like content.
It feels like a conversation you needed to have.

P.S. Who do you write to?
```

Why it works: immediately actionable on the reader's next post. The bullets show the *effect* of the technique, not the instruction. The closing line is quotable.

## Hook formulas

- **Bold claim** — "Most people fail on LinkedIn because they write for everyone."
- **Specific result** — "I went from 0 to 50K followers in 9 months. Here's the one thing that mattered."
- **Counterintuitive** — "The best content strategy I ever used was posting less."
- **Curiosity gap** — "I've studied 500+ viral posts. The ones that blow up do one thing differently."
- **Relatable pain** — "You know that feeling when you spend an hour on a post and get 3 likes?"
- **Transformation** — "I packed a one-way ticket and it changed everything."

## Re-hook patterns

- **Add heft / preempt objection** — "This is the easiest way to find clients on LinkedIn" → "without spending a single dollar"
- **Create contrast** — "LinkedIn is the #1 business platform" → "But it's the worst platform to sell your products"
- **Parenthetical qualifier** — "How I grew my audience 10x faster:" → "(Without having to post 7x a week)"

## Good vs. bad, line level

**Hook**
- Bad: "I'm excited to share some thoughts on personal branding."
- Good: "I spent 6 months building the wrong audience. Here's how I figured it out."

**Body**
- Bad: "It's really important to be authentic on LinkedIn and share your genuine experiences. Authenticity is the key to building trust."
- Good: "I posted 90 days straight. Followed every template. Got 12K followers. Zero clients. Then I wrote one honest post about a mistake I made — and 6 people DM'd me that week."

**CTA**
- Bad: "Like and share if you agree! Follow me for more content like this. Comment below and don't forget to subscribe!"
- Good: "P.S. What's one piece of advice you had to unlearn?"

**Formatting**
- Bad: one 60-word block paragraph covering niche, consistency, engagement, and relationships.
- Good:
```
3 things I'd tell myself on day 1:

1. Pick a niche so small it feels embarrassing
2. Write for 100 people, not 100,000
3. Comments > posts for your first 90 days

I ignored all three. It cost me 6 months.
```

## Additional anti-patterns

- Do not tag people for engagement farming. Tag someone only if the post is genuinely about them.
- Do not ask for "like, comment, and share." Pick one action.
- Do not write a post anyone could have written. Remove the byline — would a reader still know it was this author?
- Do not use ALL CAPS for emphasis more than once.
- Do not use negative framing in hooks. Negative hooks underperform neutral or positive ones.
- Do not put a sales pitch in the body. Use a pinned first comment if it is needed at all.
